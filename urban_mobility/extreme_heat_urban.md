# Extreme Heat & Urban Heat Island — Commuter Safety
Source: NDMA, WHO, IMD Urban Heat Action Plan

## Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect
- Cities 2–8°C warmer than surrounding rural areas due to:
  - Heat-absorbing asphalt and concrete surfaces
  - Reduced vegetation and tree cover
  - Anthropogenic heat from vehicles/AC/industry
- Worst in: Dense city cores; slum areas with low albedo roofs; industrial zones

## Feels-Like Temperature vs. Actual Temp (Hot, Humid Cities)
| Actual Temp | Humidity | Feels Like | Risk |
|------------|---------|-----------|-----|
| 35°C | 40% | ~35°C | Caution |
| 38°C | 60% | ~44°C | Danger |
| 40°C | 70% | ~52°C | Extreme Danger |
| 42°C | 80% | > 60°C | Life-Threatening |

## Commuter Heat Safety Rules
- Avoid peak heat (12 PM – 4 PM) for outdoor commuting if max > 40°C
- Use shaded covered footpaths where available
- Motorcycle riders: Full-sleeve cotton clothing reduces heat stroke risk
- Helmets: Ensure ventilated helmet use; metallic helmets absorb 3–5°C extra heat

## High-Risk Urban Occupation Groups
- Construction workers: Mandatory 30-min break per 2 hours + 250 ml water/hour
- Traffic police: Rotate shifts every 2 hours in summer; hydration mandatory
- Street vendors: Advisory to work under shade; avoid 12–4 PM slot
- Domestic workers: Employer obligation to provide cool water and shaded rest area

## City Cooling Interventions (Smart Cities)
- Mist fans at bus stops, railway stations (> 38°C trigger)
- Emergency drinking water kiosks in public areas
- Cool Roof Programme: White/reflective roof coating → reduces indoor temp by 2–4°C
- Night Shelter opening: All municipal shelters open 24/7 during heatwave alerts
